
Absolutely. In fact, if everyone in the field starts doing dyads, triads, networks, co-occurrences, embeddings, and prediction models, the next genuinely interesting move is to ask:

> **What if the divine names are not the most interesting object in the Qur'an?**

You could keep the same computational rigor and move into much deeper territories.

---

# DIRECTION A: STUDY DIVINE ACTIONS INSTEAD OF DIVINE NAMES

Most work studies what Allah is called.

But the Qur'an may reveal far more through what Allah does.

Examples:

- forgives
    
- guides
    
- creates
    
- destroys
    
- sends down
    
- teaches
    
- tests
    
- resurrects
    
- seals hearts
    
- provides
    
- knows
    
- hears
    

Construct a corpus of divine actions.

Question:

What is the action architecture of the Qur'an?

Perhaps names are secondary and actions are primary.

You may discover that the Qur'anic conception of God is much more action-centered than attribute-centered.

---

# DIRECTION B: THE COMPUTATIONAL STUDY OF DIVINE SPEECH

Instead of:

"What names are used?"

Ask:

"How does Allah speak?"

Analyze:

- commands
    
- questions
    
- oaths
    
- warnings
    
- promises
    
- rhetorical questions
    
- challenges
    
- narratives
    

Example:

How often does Allah persuade versus command?

How often does Allah reason versus assert?

This opens computational rhetoric.

---

# DIRECTION C: THE STRUCTURE OF ARGUMENTS

The Qur'an is full of arguments.

Yet surprisingly little computational work studies them.

Map argument structures.

Example:

Claim:

Allah is one.

Evidence:

Creation.

Inference:

Therefore worship Him.

Build argument graphs.

Question:

What are the most common reasoning patterns?

This connects Qur'anic studies to computational argumentation.

---

# DIRECTION D: HUMAN PSYCHOLOGY IN THE QUR'AN

This is a huge unexplored area.

Extract every statement about:

- fear
    
- hope
    
- arrogance
    
- envy
    
- gratitude
    
- forgetfulness
    
- impatience
    
- denial
    

Then build a computational model.

Questions:

What is the Qur'an's theory of human failure?

What psychological traits cluster together?

You could reconstruct an entire Qur'anic psychology.

---

# DIRECTION E: THE COMPUTATIONAL ANTHROPOLOGY OF THE QUR'AN

Forget God for a moment.

Study humans.

Construct networks of human types:

- believers
    
- hypocrites
    
- disbelievers
    
- prophets
    
- rulers
    
- oppressed
    
- scholars
    

Then analyze transitions.

Question:

What makes someone move from one category to another?

This becomes a model of Qur'anic anthropology.

# DIRECTION F: MORAL CAUSALITY

One of the most fascinating possibilities.

The Qur'an constantly implies causal chains:

Arrogance  
→ blindness

Repentance  
→ mercy

Gratitude  
→ increase

Injustice  
→ destruction

Extract these systematically.

Build a causal network.

You would essentially reconstruct the Qur'an's moral universe.

# DIRECTION K: THE COMPUTATIONAL STUDY OF QUESTIONS

The Qur'an asks hundreds of questions.

Examples:

# DIRECTION L: THE COMPUTATIONAL STUDY OF SILENCE

This sounds strange but is profound.

What topics are conspicuously underdeveloped?

What expected details are omitted?

Example:

Many narratives lack details modern readers expect.

Question:

What is the rhetorical function of omission?

Absence is data.

# DIRECTION M: NARRATIVE ENGINEERING

Study stories as systems.

Questions:

What narrative components recur?

- warning
    
- rejection
    
- punishment
    
- rescue
    

Can all prophetic narratives be decomposed into reusable modules?

This would resemble computational narratology.

# DIRECTION N: THE QUR'ANIC THEORY OF FAILURE

Every destroyed nation failed somehow.

Extract all failure causes.

Cluster them.

You may discover a small number of recurring failure mechanisms:

- arrogance
    
- corruption
    
- denial
    
- injustice
    
- excess
    

This becomes a computational sociology of collapse.

---

# DIRECTION O: THE QUR'ANIC THEORY OF SUCCESS

Similarly:

Why do people succeed?

Extract:

- patience
    
- gratitude
    
- trust
    
- sincerity
    
- repentance
    

Construct a success network.

Then compare success and failure architectures.

---

# DIRECTION P: CONCEPT EVOLUTION

Track concepts across the Qur'an.

Example:

How does the notion of "faith" change across revelation chronology?

Or:

How does "jannah" evolve?

This is semantic evolution analysis.

---

# DIRECTION Q: CHARACTER NETWORKS

People have done simple versions of this.

But not deeply.

Build a network where nodes are:

- prophets
    
- nations
    
- rulers
    
- communities
    

Then analyze structural roles.

Who functions as bridges?

Who functions as archetypes?

---

# DIRECTION R: THE QUR'AN AS A DECISION SYSTEM

This may be the most ambitious.

Imagine every human situation as a state.

The Qur'an provides transitions.

Example:

Sin  
→ Repent

Oppression  
→ Resist

Prosperity  
→ Gratitude

Build a state-transition model.

The Qur'an becomes a behavioral operating system.

---

# DIRECTION S: THE QUR'ANIC MODEL OF REALITY

This is perhaps the deepest possible project.

Instead of studying names, themes, stories, or words:

Reconstruct the ontology of the Qur'an.

What entities exist?

- Allah
    
- angels
    
- humans
    
- jinn
    
- souls
    
- nations
    
- heavens
    
- earth
    
- paradise
    
- hell
    

What relations connect them?

Essentially:

Build a formal map of reality according to the Qur'an.

This would be closer to knowledge representation and ontology engineering than traditional Qur'anic studies.

1. **Moral Causality Networks** (how actions lead to outcomes).
    
2. **Qur'anic Psychology** (architecture of human traits and emotions).
    
3. **Qur'anic Epistemology** (how truth is known).
    
4. **Divine Actions Rather Than Divine Names**.
    
5. **Argument Structure Mining**.
    
6. **Theory of Civilizational Collapse** (destroyed nations corpus).
    
7. **Success/Failure State-Transition Models**.
    
8. **Ontology of Reality in the Qur'an**.
    
9. **Narrative Engineering of Prophetic Stories**.
    
10. **The Qur'an as a Dynamic Decision System**.
    

These move beyond "attribute studies" and toward reconstructing entire cognitive, moral, social, and metaphysical systems embedded in the text. That's where computational Qur'anic studies can become genuinely interdisciplinary rather than remaining a niche subfield of lexical analysis.
