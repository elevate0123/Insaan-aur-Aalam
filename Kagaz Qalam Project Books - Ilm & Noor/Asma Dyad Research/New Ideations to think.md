

The challenge is that the 90 ideas are not 90 separate projects. They are really **different levels of the same research program**.

A coherent research agenda would look something like this:

---

# LEVEL 1: DESCRIPTIVE STRUCTURE

## "What patterns exist?"

This is where most current studies stop.

The goal is simply to map the observable system.

### A. Frequency Analysis

Questions:

- Which names occur most?
    
- Which dyads occur most?
    
- Which are rare?
    

Example:

Suppose:

- al-Ghafūr appears 90 times.
    
- al-Raḥīm appears 120 times.
    
- al-Ghafūr al-Raḥīm appears 72 times.
    

This immediately suggests dependency.

But frequency alone explains nothing.

It only identifies candidates for deeper investigation.

---

### B. Network Analysis

Construct:

Node = Divine Name

Edge = Co-occurrence

Example:

```
Ghafūr ----- Raḥīm
   |
Tawwāb
```

versus

```
ʿAzīz ----- Ḥakīm
   |
Qawiyy
```

Now ask:

Are there clusters?

Do mercy names form one region?

Do power names form another?

Do some names bridge clusters?

This reveals architecture.

Not theology.

Architecture.

---

### C. Order Analysis

Why:

- Ghafūr → Raḥīm
    

instead of

- Raḥīm → Ghafūr
    

For every dyad:

Measure directionality.

The order itself is data.

A language rarely preserves order accidentally.

---

# LEVEL 2: CONTEXTUAL STRUCTURE

## "What conditions trigger particular names?"

Now move beyond names themselves.

Ask what causes them.

---

### A. Theme → Name Mapping

Build categories:

- repentance
    
- law
    
- creation
    
- eschatology
    
- prophecy
    

Then measure:

```
Theme → Divine Name
```

Example:

Repentance passages may strongly predict:

- Ghafūr
    
- Raḥīm
    
- Tawwāb
    

Legal passages may predict:

- ʿAzīz
    
- Ḥakīm
    

Now names become responses to contexts.

---

### B. Audience Analysis

Identify:

Who is being addressed?

- believers
    
- disbelievers
    
- hypocrites
    
- prophets
    
- humanity
    

Question:

Does the same attribute appear differently depending on audience?

Example:

Knowledge attributes may occur differently when addressing hypocrites than believers.

This suggests audience-sensitive attribute selection.

---

### C. Psychological-State Analysis

Infer emotional states:

- fear
    
- hope
    
- arrogance
    
- despair
    

Then ask:

What attributes are deployed to regulate each state?

Example:

Despair → Mercy names

Arrogance → Power names

This treats names as psychological interventions.

---

# LEVEL 3: FUNCTIONAL ANALYSIS

## "What job is the divine name performing?"

This is where things become much more interesting.

---

### A. Reinforcement Theory

Traditional assumption:

The name reinforces the passage.

Example:

Passage about forgiveness.

Ending:

Ghafūr Raḥīm.

Simple alignment.

---

### B. Compensation Theory

A more interesting possibility.

Example:

Passage contains harsh warnings.

Yet ends:

Ghafūr Raḥīm.

Why?

Maybe the name balances rather than reinforces.

This is a major hypothesis.

---

### C. Interpretive-Key Theory

The name may tell the reader:

"Read the preceding passage through this lens."

Example:

A legal ruling ending with:

ʿAzīz Ḥakīm

may imply:

Authority + Wisdom

is the interpretive framework.

The dyad becomes a hermeneutic instruction.

---

# LEVEL 4: THE GRAMMAR OF DIVINE ATTRIBUTES

## "Why are some combinations allowed and others absent?"

This is one of the richest directions.

---

### The Basic Observation

Suppose there are roughly 99 names.

Potential pairings:

~4,851

Observed pairings:

Only a tiny subset.

Question:

Why?

---

### Example

Common:

- Ghafūr Raḥīm
    

Never observed:

- Mutakabbir Raḥīm
    
- Jabbar Latīf
    

Why not?

Coincidence?

Or grammar?

---

### Research Goal

Infer rules:

```
Power names pair with wisdom names.
Mercy names pair with forgiveness names.
Knowledge names pair with awareness names.
```

This becomes:

A syntax of attributes.

---

# LEVEL 5: COUNTERFACTUAL ANALYSIS

## "Why this dyad rather than another?"

Most studies never ask this.

They merely explain what exists.

---

### Example

Verse ends:

ʿAzīz Ḥakīm

Generate alternatives:

- Ghafūr Raḥīm
    
- ʿAlīm Khabīr
    
- Qawiyy Matīn
    

Now compare.

Which fits best?

Why?

Measure:

- semantic coherence
    
- thematic coherence
    
- rhetorical coherence
    

This is extremely powerful.

---

### Core Question

Not:

Why does ʿAzīz Ḥakīm occur?

But:

Why does every alternative perform worse?

That is a much stronger question.

---

# LEVEL 6: PREDICTIVE MODELING

## "Can we predict the divine name before seeing it?"

This is where research becomes scientific.

---

### Procedure

Remove all divine-name endings.

Train a model.

Input:

Verse.

Output:

Predicted dyad.

---

### Example

Model reads:

- repentance
    
- forgiveness
    
- return to God
    

Prediction:

Ghafūr Raḥīm.

If accuracy is high:

Names are systematically selected.

Not random.

---

### Explainability

Use SHAP or similar methods.

Ask:

What words caused prediction?

Maybe:

- tawbah
    
- dhanb
    
- forgiveness
    

strongly predict Ghafūr.

Now hidden relationships become visible.

---

# LEVEL 7: HIGHER-ORDER STRUCTURES

## "Are dyads even the right unit?"

This is where many future discoveries may lie.

---

### Triads

Instead of:

Name A + Name B

Study:

Theme + Name A + Name B

Example:

```
Repentance
+
Ghafūr
+
Raḥīm
```

The real structure may be triadic.

---

### Hypergraphs

Nodes:

- themes
    
- names
    
- audiences
    
- discourse states
    

Then analyze simultaneously.

This captures complexity lost in pairwise studies.

---

# LEVEL 8: DYNAMIC SYSTEMS

## "How do attributes move through discourse?"

Most studies are static.

The Qur'an is dynamic.

---

### Attribute Trajectories

A surah may move:

```
Mercy
→ Warning
→ Judgment
→ Mercy
```

Track attribute shifts.

Treat names as states.

---

### Phase Transitions

Detect abrupt changes.

Example:

A narrative suddenly shifts from:

Mercy cluster

to

Power cluster.

Why?

What triggered transition?

---

### Control-System Hypothesis

Perhaps attributes regulate discourse.

Maintaining balance between:

- fear and hope
    
- justice and mercy
    
- transcendence and intimacy
    

This is a sophisticated systems-theory approach.

---

# LEVEL 9: THEOLOGY OF ABSENCE

## "What does not occur?"

This is massively underexplored.

---

### Missing Dyads

Generate all possible combinations.

Compare:

Possible  
vs  
Observed

Study missing combinations.

---

### Why Important?

In linguistics:

Impossible sentences reveal grammar.

In genetics:

Missing mutations reveal constraints.

Similarly:

Absent divine pairings may reveal theological rules.

---

# LEVEL 10: GRAND THEORY

## "What mechanism generated the entire system?"

This is the endpoint.

Everything else feeds into this.

---

### Competing Theories

#### Theory 1

Names merely describe God.

Weak theory.

---

#### Theory 2

Names summarize passage content.

Better.

---

#### Theory 3

Names guide interpretation.

Stronger.

---

#### Theory 4

Names regulate audience psychology.

Even stronger.

---

#### Theory 5

Names are outputs of an optimization process.

Strongest.

Input:

- topic
    
- audience
    
- emotional state
    
- rhetorical goal
    
- discourse structure
    

Output:

optimal dyad.

---

# The Ultimate Research Question

A mature research program eventually stops asking:

> Why does al-Ghafūr al-Raḥīm occur?

and starts asking:

> Given the context, could any other dyad have worked equally well?

If the answer is consistently "no," then you are no longer studying frequency patterns.

You are studying a highly constrained selection system.

And that, in my view, is where the most original computational work on Qur'anic divine-name dyads is likely to emerge: **from co-occurrence statistics, to attribute grammar, to counterfactual testing, to predictive modeling, and finally to a theory of divine-name selection.**
